02

The motivation behind
working in this sector

History, literature and lived experience have taught me that there have always been aspects of our lives, our society, our culture and our politics that lack rightful representation — aspects that could resist the prevailing hegemonic discourses. I would like to work towards bringing those to the reading public.

Someone has to decide that a testimony deserves a print run, a price and a place in a catalogue. Until that decision is taken, it stays a private document.

I would like to be among the people who take it — and who make sure the telling survives the teller.

03

Different operations, and the different
demands, of the publishing industry

A manuscript does not publish itself. It requires editorial, development, strategy and marketing — different operations under different names — before it can become publishable, and at last available to a reading public.

Advances in technology, AI models included, cannot replace the human creativity this industry demands. Can they augment it? Maybe!

What no model can do is decide what deserves to be made, or answer to the author whose work it is. Those remain human judgements, taken by named people at named desks.

At the editorial desk

  • fact-check every claim
  • structure the manuscript
  • commission a reader’s report
  • clear permissions & credits
  • line-edit, then proof

At the development desk

  • shape the brief
  • query the author
  • rebuild the chapters
  • apparatus, notes & index
  • comparable titles, schedule, specs

Writing has always been my way of making sense of the world — its silences, its violence, and its quiet moments of grace. These poems emerge from lived experience, from the margins, and from a deep belief in the power of words to witness, resist, and heal.

I archive them out. I know the shape of the wait from the inside — which is a qualification, not a hobby, for an editor or developer. A creator’s work that has never been edited or developed by someone else has never been published.

05

Not mere enthusiasm.
Trained hands.

Published materials exist only because, beyond the first author, many others wanted them printed or digitised properly. I bring to the industry research training, editorial training, training in Bengali and English translation, teaching experience, and a rejection folder of my own.

Three questions stand between a manuscript and a published text. Is it true? Is it needed? How will it be remembered by its readers? I would like to be one of the people who help answer each of them.
